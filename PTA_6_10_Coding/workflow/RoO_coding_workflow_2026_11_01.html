Coding Workflow for RoO Restricted-Input Pairs

General coding rules for every preferential trade agreement (PTA). The definition of an input–output link is expansive: every pair starts at DV = 1, and each step turns to 0 only the cases it clearly excludes. Pairs are written input → output (A → B). The rules include the scope decisions of 19 October 2026 and the conventions of 28–30 October 2026 (Professor Zhang’s questionnaire answers), with the points he settled on 1 November 2026; all are summarised in the last section. Results for individual PTAs are reported separately.

Decision rule

DV is the main variable (Professor Zhang, 1 November 2026). A pair is turned to 0 only on clear evidence that the input cannot be used to make the output: a step or convention below says 0, the direction is reversed, or the claimed route is physically or chemically impossible or too generic to name. A pair that cannot be settled stays 1 and is marked uncertain; it is never turned to 0 for being uncertain. Every pair that stays 1 falls into one of two levels:

LevelMeaningCode
KnownA known production route of the output (or of the same kind of product) uses the input, or standard industrial practice doesDV = 1, uncertain = 0
PossibleTechnically possible, but no known route confirms itDV = 1, uncertain = 1
InvalidPhysically or chemically impossible, the reverse direction, or no specific route can be namedDV = 0

The three levels are Professor Zhang’s overall principle of 30 October. The uncertain flag records which 1s are only possible; no variant of DV that counts only certain links is computed (1 November). Accuracy is reported for DV.

Scope. The conventions of 28–30 October apply to the pairs newly coded in PTA 6–10 and to all later agreements. Codes delivered for PTA 1–5 are not re-judged (Professor Zhang, 30 October). Pairs copied from them keep their codes (step 7a), including errors an audit finds in them, so a pair has the same code in every agreement. Two updates are pending for the next reissue of the PTA 1–5 files (Professor Zhang, 1 November): DV_strict is dropped from them, and under the blended-fabric convention the copied fabric pairs that share a minor fibre get uncertain = 1. In PTA 6–10 the conventions were applied by rule to whole classes; the answers that would raise single pairs from 0 to 1 and the distinctions between a certain 1 and a 1 with uncertainty were not re-judged pair by pair (see the results report). They apply in full to pairs not yet in the master list. Pairs already in it are copied as they are (step 7a), including PTA 6–10 pairs to which these answers were not applied (for example 842420 spray guns → 842410 fire extinguishers = 0). They are not re-reviewed; in particular, the convention on complete machines is not applied to pairs both coders coded 0 (Professor Zhang, 1 November).

Step 1. Take in the RoO pairs and set DV = 1

  • Read each agreement’s product-specific rules and list every (restricted input, output) pair at HS6, in the agreement’s own HS vintage.
  • Only HS6 inputs are coded. Rows whose input is not an HS code — “WO” (wholly obtained) or “any” — are deleted.
  • Map every pair to a common HS vintage (HS2002) with the WCO/UN correlation tables. A code that splits produces one pair per new code; flag non-one-to-one mappings as concord_flag = 1. Where two codes of the agreement fall into the same HS2002 code, the pair is coded on the agreement’s own codes and descriptions. Any other record that maps to several HS2002 pairs takes the highest DV among them, with uncertain = 1 if they differ (unless input and output are the same code, which step 2 locks).
  • A code that is not a valid subheading is mapped to every HS2002 subheading of its group and flagged.
  • Remove duplicate pairs within each agreement and keep a pair-level master list across agreements (used in step 7).
  • Set DV = 1 for every pair. Steps 3–6 can only turn a 1 into a 0; only a convention for a whole class of pairs (see “Decided by rule” under the two AI coders) can turn a 0 back into a 1.

The HS concordance will follow the project’s HS conversion package once it is adopted; the rules above apply until then.

Step 2. Lock identical or same-good pairs at 1

Rule: set locked = 1 (route = same_good) when input and output are the same good or very similar: the same HS6 code, or the same species, material or variety differing only in processing stage, preservation state, packing or size of presentation, or a treatment the producer can add (roasting, decaffeinating, dyeing, printing, plying). Locked pairs skip steps 3–6.

Boundary with siblings (step 4). “Very similar” means one can be turned into the other. Goods that differ in a fixed attribute — fibre, species, gender cut, base material — are siblings, not the same good: women’s cotton jackets → women’s synthetic jackets is not locked and goes to 0 at step 4. The direction must also be possible: dyed fabric → unbleached fabric is not locked and goes to 0 at step 5.

Size or format (30 October). When the same good comes in another size, lock only the feasible direction: wide → narrow and long → short film can be slit or cut. The reverse (narrow → wide film, a short roll of film → a long roll) is 0 at step 5. Intermediate goods such as paper and paperboard in rolls or sheets are not affected; they stay 1 as intermediate siblings (step 4). Endless belts of another circumference, or belts that differ in reinforcement, are different goods (0).

Step 3. Turn to 0: clearly irrelevant pairs

Rule: turn to 0 only when no route from step 6 could apply: the input would have to change into a different species, plant, fibre or substance, and it is not a constituent, feed, part or waste of the output. If in doubt, leave the pair at 1 for step 6.

Caution. Do not turn a pair to 0 at this step when the output is a mixture, a farmed animal or a waste product; leave it at 1 for step 6. Example: chicken cuts → 160249 (prepared pork and mixtures) looks irrelevant but is a mixture pair.

Fibres. Different synthetic fibres — nylon, polyester, acrylic, polypropylene — count as one fibre: nylon yarn → acrylic yarn is not a step-3 pair; it is an intermediate sibling (step 4) and stays 1. Different natural or artificial fibres (cotton → polyester, flax → jute, viscose → polyester) remain different fibres. Blended fabrics (30 October): fabrics of different main fibres that share a minor fibre, or where the input’s main fibre is the output’s minor fibre (polyester/cotton → viscose/cotton), are 1 with uncertainty.

Fish and other seafood. Different species are different goods: the pair is 0 at this step unless the input could feed the output (step 6, feed). The intermediate-sibling rule of step 4 does not apply to different species.

Parallel by-products (30 October)

Two parallel by-products of the same source, neither of which is made from or yields the other, are not inputs of each other: 020329 frozen pork → 050210 pig bristles is 0 (step 3). Bristles come from slaughter, not from processing pork. Waste routes that do stay 1 are in step 6.

Step 6. Turn to 0 only where the expansive definition finds no route

Rule: every pair still at 1 is checked against the expansive routes below. It stays 1 if any route plausibly applies (record the route). It is turned to 0 only if you are sure no route exists — the cases listed in each table.

General principle. Use the broadest scope: keep 1 wherever any production route is technically possible, even if it is uncommon. A known route is a certain 1; a route that is possible but unconfirmed is 1 with uncertainty (decision rule above). If in doubt, keep 1.

Two AI coders and conflicts

  • Setup. Two independent coders from different model families (Claude and Codex) receive the same rules (steps 2–6 with these examples) and the same pairs. Neither sees the other’s answer.
  • Units. To code tens of thousands of pairs, pairs are grouped by heading pair (input HS4 × output HS4). Each coder gives one default code per unit plus exceptions for single input codes, output codes or pairs, with the 5 most similar coded pairs as worked examples.
  • Question asked. Each coder decides whether the pair should be turned from 1 to 0, and at which step, and returns {DV, route, step, one-sentence reason}. For a 1 it also returns the level: known or possible (decision rule).
  • Combining rule. Both say 0: DV = 0. Both say 1: DV = 1, with uncertain = 1 if either coder rated the link only possible. If they disagree, the pair stays 1 with uncertain = 1, and both codes and reasons are kept.
  • Decided by rule. Where a convention (last section) settles a pair, the pair is coded by that convention, with the uncertainty the convention gives. Conventions for whole classes — metal articles of the same metal (step 4) and the levels of direct remelting (step 6, for semi-finished goods and articles as inputs) — apply even when both coders said 0.
  • Screen of one-sided 1s. A one-sided 1 that no convention settles is screened for clear invalidity: it fails step 3, is physically or chemically impossible, runs in the reverse direction, names only a generic process role, or is a parallel by-product. One model family screens every such pair; the other screens, blind, the pairs the first found invalid. Only pairs that both find clearly invalid become 0 (example: tobacco → garments). All others stay 1 with uncertainty; for chemicals, a 1 stays whenever a specific role can be named (Professor Zhang, 1 November).
  • Applying a new convention to pairs already coded. A convention applied by rule can turn a 1 to 0. Those pairs are screened once for another specific route (a component, ingredient or forward use); if one exists, the pair is 1 with uncertainty. A pair the convention’s own example decides is not reopened, and neither is any plastics → plastics pair (extension (iv) of step 6).
  • Reliability. Report Cohen’s kappa between the two coders. Then code a stratified sample of about 300 pairs across sources, steps and routes to estimate the accuracy of the final codes, weighting each stratum by its size, by pairs and by records, with 95% intervals. Without a domain expert, the sample is coded by two new AI sessions that see only the rules and the pairs (no earlier answer), and a person reviews every pair where they disagree with each other or with the final code. The true value of a sampled pair follows the conventions in force; when a convention changes, the previous version is re-scored on the same true values. Errors that form a group are corrected by rule in every PTA not yet delivered, not pair by pair; audit codes do not change a final code pair by pair. (One exception, in PTA 6–10 only: 6 sampled pairs that both auditors coded 0, in classes where Professor Zhang said such pairs are 0, were set to 0; accuracy is also reported counting them as errors.) Report it as an independent AI audit, not as expert hand-coding.

Step 7. Scale up

(a) Exact matches

Any pair already in the coded master list (same HS2002 pair after concordance) takes the existing DV, route and uncertain values, with source = exact_copy. The master list grows with every coded PTA. Exact copies are re-copied whenever the master labels are corrected, so a pair has the same code in every PTA.

(b) Train a model on the coded pairs

  • Features: input and output chapters and headings; same-chapter and same-heading flags; flags from the step 2, 4 and 5 rules; text embeddings of both descriptions and their interaction.
  • Target: whether the pair stays 1 or is turned to 0 (optionally also route and step).
  • Validation: hold out whole chapter pairs (grouped cross-validation) so the model cannot memorise near-duplicate pairs; random cross-validation overstates accuracy.

(c) Apply with confidence bands

Predicted probabilityAction
≥ 0.9Keep at 1 (source = ml)
≤ 0.1Turn to 0 (source = ml)
Between 0.1 and 0.9Send through steps 2–6 with the two AI coders, giving them the 5 most similar coded pairs as worked examples

Support condition. Use the bands only where the chapter pair has more than 30 coded pairs in the master list, and only for pair types where the audit (d) shows the bands are reliable. Other pairs go to the AI coders or to explicit rules. So far the bands have been used only for textile pairs confirmed by the textile rules.

(d) Audit

Check a random sample of model-coded pairs from each band against the two AI coders (or a person). If accuracy in a band falls below the threshold, narrow that band and send more pairs to the AI coders. When the model and an explicit rule disagree, the rule is applied where it finds a route; otherwise the pair goes to the AI coders.

(e) Explicit rules for structured chapters

Textile chapters 50–63 are coded by explicit rules. Each code has a stage (fibre, yarn, fabric, trim, made-up or garment, waste), a fibre and, for fabrics and garments, a construction (knitted or not). The rules apply steps 2–6 and the conventions above: forward stage with the same fibre = 1; minor constituents as in step 6; finished siblings = 0; backward = 0; intermediate siblings of one fibre = 1; sewing thread and trims → articles = component; waste reprocessed = 1; yarn, fabric or fibre → worn clothing (6309) and fibre → rags (6310) = 1. The textile rules do not yet code the blended fabrics of step 3 (different main fibres sharing a minor fibre, 1 with uncertainty); they are being extended before the next agreements are coded. In PTA 6–10 every such pair was a copy.

Chapter 59. Wicks (5908) and textile hosepiping (5909) → transmission belting (5910) = 0; coated or rubberised fabric (5901, 5903, 5906, 5907) → belting = 1; 5901 → tyre cord fabric (5902) = 1.

Reading “of other textile materials”. In the HS this means every fibre that is not named in the same group of subheadings. 610990 T-shirts “of other textile materials” include synthetic T-shirts, because only cotton has its own subheading (610910), so 540231 nylon yarn → 610990 is a main-fibre (forward) pair. 620449 dresses of other textile materials exclude wool, cotton, synthetic and artificial fibres, which have their own subheadings.

Example. Another agreement restricts 030342 frozen yellowfin tuna → 160414 prepared tuna; the exact pair is already coded, so it is copied and stays 1 (forward). A new pair, 731815 steel bolts → 730511 welded line pipe, goes to the two AI coders; both find only the shared material, so by the same-material table it is 1 with uncertain = 1. A third pair, 230120 fishmeal → 020714 chicken cuts: one coder names fishmeal as poultry feed, the other codes 0; the screen does not find the claim clearly invalid, so it stays DV = 1 with uncertain = 1.
